\lettrine{T}{his} project set out to address a concrete tension in
applying large language models to regulatory compliance: the same
unstructured-document reasoning that makes an LLM attractive for this
task is exactly what makes it untrustworthy once it is allowed anywhere
near a number that determines a real financial liability. Rather than
avoiding LLMs altogether, this project kept the two concerns
architecturally separate: a deterministic, independently verifiable
calculation engine on one side, and a genuinely autonomous, ReAct-style
tool-use agent on the other, wired together through a two-machine,
VPN-meshed deployment and integrated into a working Odoo module.

The evaluation turned up a finding more nuanced than a single accuracy
number. The dominant constraint on agentic compliance extraction turned
out to be a small local model's own tool-calling discipline, not the
orchestration architecture built around it --- and, just as importantly,
a model's apparent reliability depends heavily on how it is measured,
rather than being some fixed property of the model itself. The project's
own raw benchmark initially and wrongly dismissed a model that, once
re-evaluated against the real execution harness, completed 8 of 10 jobs
where the benchmark had predicted none, with field accuracy equal to the
presumed leader's. \textbf{This methodology-sensitivity of model ranking is one of
this work's central findings}: a benchmark that does not match the real
system's execution semantics can actively mislead an architectural
decision, and only empirical re-verification against the production path
--- not a one-off benchmark score --- earns the right to trust a model
choice. Pushing that same discipline further, at full and honest dataset
scale rather than on a flattering subsample, the project went on to find
a larger candidate model and a hybrid deterministic/regex-plus-LLM
extraction path. Together they raised measured field accuracy from a
0\% baseline to 64--86\% on plumbing-reachable fields and 76--81\% on the
two fields requiring genuine document comprehension --- fields that, for
most of this project's evaluation history, looked like a hard ceiling on
what the model could do.

Looking ahead, the two most important next steps are measuring the agentic
loop against a single-shot extraction baseline on the same harness, and
testing on real supplier documents; both would turn the current results
from strong evidence on synthetic data into general claims. Beyond those,
this work leaves a clear path forward: re-validating the
two most recent targeted fixes at the same full dataset scale the rest of
the evaluation insists on; resolving the identified interaction between
the single-write design and the source-quote grounding gate;
benchmarking additional and fine-tuned models against the same
harness-matched methodology; extending the audit trail into a
human-review interface that surfaces grounding confidence per field; and
restoring the originally specified per-role network topology once its
external blocker is resolved. Because deterministic calculation and
agentic extraction were kept architecturally separate from the start,
each of these is an incremental extension rather than a rebuild. The
foundation is in place; what remains is to make the extraction layer as
trustworthy as the calculation layer already is.
